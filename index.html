How to get a HAR file

  1. In Chrome, press F12, open the Network tab and tick Preserve log.
  2. Clear the log, then visit the pages you want to study.
  3. Right click the request list and choose Save all as HAR.
  4. Drop the file here. Repeat with a second browsing session to compare.

    How Tracker Lens works

    A website is rarely built by one company alone. This page shows who else was involved in your visit.

    What happens when you open a website

    Your browser loads the page from the website itself, and then loads more pieces from other companies: ads, statistics, fonts, videos, share buttons. Every one of these requests tells that company which page you are on, and often comes with a cookie that recognises your browser again later.

    Most of this happens out of sight. The page looks the same whether it talked to two companies or eighty.

    What Tracker Lens counts

    For every page in your file, it counts the outside domains: the different websites, other than the one you opened, that your browser contacted. Addresses are grouped by their main domain, so securepubads.g.doubleclick.net and ad.doubleclick.net count once, as doubleclick.net.

    It counts companies rather than requests on purpose. One ad server can send fifty requests, but it is still one company that learned about the visit.

    What a HAR file is

    A HAR file (HTTP Archive) is a recording of everything your browser loaded, saved from the browser's developer tools. It lists every request with its address and timing. Tracker Lens reads only the addresses and which page they belong to.

    Comparing two sessions

    One file shows how a set of websites behaves. Two files show what a change does. Record the same websites twice and change one thing in between, for example:

    • accepting all cookies versus rejecting them in the consent banner
    • a normal browser versus one with an ad blocker
    • a fresh browser profile versus one that has been browsing for a week

    Tracker Lens then matches the websites across both files and reports the change for each one. Websites that appear in only one file are left out of the comparison, because a different website is not a fair comparison.

    What it cannot tell you

    • What the companies did with the information. A contacted domain may be a harmless font server or an ad network building a profile.
    • Who owns a domain. Several domains can belong to one company.
    • Anything your browser did not load. Data shared between companies' servers is invisible here.

    Results also vary from visit to visit, because ads and tracking scripts change with time and demand. Record each session more than once before you draw conclusions.

    Your data

    The file is read inside this browser tab and never sent anywhere. The page works offline too. Be careful when sharing HAR files with other people: they can contain the addresses you visited and, in some browsers, cookies or form data.
